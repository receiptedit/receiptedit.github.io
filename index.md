# What a Valid Receipt Must Contain (A Practical Checklist)

A receipt is only useful if it can answer a later question: what was paid, by whom, to whom, when, and for what. That question might come from a customer, a bookkeeper, a tax preparer, or from you, months after the sale, when the day is no longer clear.

This checklist is a practical starting point for receipts you issue yourself — a shop sale, a service payment, a rent payment, a cash sale at a stall. It is not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Rules vary by country and by type of transaction, so treat local requirements as the final word.

## The details that should appear on almost every receipt

**1. Your business name and enough contact detail to identify you.**  
A trading name on its own is weak if a customer has two vendors with similar names. Add an address, an email, or another official identifier you actually use. If your area requires a business or tax number on sales documents, include that too.

**2. The other party's name, when the record needs it.**  
A quick retail sale may only need a receipt number and the amount. A freelance project, a rent payment, a service call, or any payment you might have to find later should name the customer, tenant, or client.

**3. The date of the payment, not only the date you typed the document.**  
If you issue the receipt the next morning, keep the payment date accurate and, if it helps, note the issue date separately. For rent periods, hotel stays, or work that spans more than one day, write the period as well as the payment date.

**4. A receipt or transaction number you can search for.**  
A simple rising sequence (R-1042, R-1043) is enough. Do not reuse a number, even if the first version had a typo — issue a corrected copy that refers to the original number instead.

**5. A plain description of what was paid for.**  
"Services" or "goods" is too thin. "Replaced kitchen faucet and supply lines, 14 Nov 2026" or "November rent, Unit 4B, 12 Oak Street" will still make sense next year. List each item or service on its own line when the payment covers more than one thing.

**6. The amount paid, and the math that leads to it.**  
Show the line amounts, any discount, tax, and the total. If the customer paid in cash, record the amount tendered and the change. If they paid only part of a larger bill, show the amount received and the balance still due. Check the figures before you hand the receipt over.

**7. How the payment was made.**  
Cash, card, bank transfer, cheque, or another method. For a transfer, a short reference or confirmation number can settle later confusion. For a card payment, you generally do not need the full card number — the method and a confirmation, or the last four digits, are enough.

**8. Tax, if tax applies.**  
Where you charge sales tax, VAT, or GST, show the rate or the tax amount as its own line rather than burying it in the total. If the sale is not taxable, a short note such as "no tax charged" prevents someone from wondering whether the line was forgotten.

## Details that matter in particular situations

- **Meals, travel, and similar business expenses.** A later review is easier if the receipt also states the business purpose, not only the vendor name and the total.
- **Rent.** Name the property, the tenant, the rental period the payment covers, and whether the amount includes utilities, late fees, or a partial payment.
- **Services and trades.** Add the job address if it is different from the billing address, and any licence or certificate reference the work required.
- **Donations.** Name the organisation, the donor, the date, the amount (or a description of a non-cash gift), and whether any goods or services were provided in return. Some tax authorities set extra wording for gifts over a stated amount; check that wording before you issue the receipt.
- **Deposits.** Say that the payment is a deposit, what it is held against, and the conditions for return or deduction. Do not let a deposit receipt look like a final sale.

## What a receipt is not

A receipt confirms a payment that already happened. An invoice asks for a payment that is still due. If you send an invoice and later receive the money, mark the invoice paid or issue a separate receipt — and keep both. It should agree with contracts, warranties, and bank statements, not contradict them.

## A short process that keeps receipts consistent

1. Use the same layout each time, so you do not skip a field under pressure.
2. Fill in the details of the real transaction. A template is only a starting point.
3. Check the total, the date, and the spelling of names in a preview before you send or print the file.
4. Give one copy to the other party and keep one for yourself.
5. File your copy by date and by customer or job, with the receipt number in the file name. `2026-11-24_R-1042_unit-4b-rent.pdf` is easy to search; `receipt-final-final.pdf` is not.
